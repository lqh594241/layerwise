A useful estimate, clearly explained.

Terms and assumptions · Updated November 5, 2026

Purpose

Layerwise helps you estimate 3D printing costs and prepare indicative quotes. It does not promise sales, profitability, print success or a market-appropriate selling price. Review your inputs and final quote before relying on them.

Units and assumptions

  • All inputs are per finished part. Filament is entered in grams with a price per kilogram; resin is entered in milliliters with a price per liter.
  • Energy cost uses average power in watts multiplied by print time and electricity rate per kilowatt-hour.
  • The failure allowance assumes failed attempts consume a full attempt's material, electricity and machine time. Labor and other costs are charged once per successful part.
  • Quantity multiplies the per-part estimate. It does not model build-plate packing, bulk efficiencies or volume discounts.
  • Percentage selling fees are applied to selling price. Fixed fees are applied per item. Actual platform fee rules may differ.
  • Target margin means profit after the entered selling fees divided by selling price. Markup means profit as a percentage of production cost, after the entered fees are recovered.
  • Suggested unit selling prices round up to the nearest cent; quantity totals use that rounded unit price. Displayed cost components may differ from the displayed total by a cent due to independent rounding.
  • Currency selection changes labels only. It does not convert values. Enter every price and rate in the same selected currency.
  • Estimates exclude taxes, shipping, demand, design work and any expense you have not entered. Add design or post-processing labor to the labor field and allocate other expenses yourself.

Examples and availability

Material presets, hourly costs and all default inputs are illustrative examples, not live market prices or recommended rates. The tool is provided as available without warranties of accuracy or uninterrupted access. Verify important calculations independently.

Downloaded quotes

A generated quote is a starting document. It does not establish a contract, payment obligation, delivery commitment or tax invoice. Agree any scope, shipping, taxes and delivery terms with your customer separately.
