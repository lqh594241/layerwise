01 / THE REAL COST

More than a spool of plastic.

Materials and machine time are only the start. Add electricity, hands-on labor, finishing supplies and an allowance for failed prints.

Print costs ÷ (1 − failure rate)
+ labor + other costs
02 / A HEALTHY MARGIN

Margin and markup aren't twins.

A 30% markup on a $10 cost gives a $13 price. A 30% profit margin needs a $14.29 price before fees. We calculate both.

Price = (cost + fixed fee)
÷ (1 − margin − fee rate)

BEFORE YOU HIT PRINT

A few good
questions.

The details behind the digits.

How do I calculate the cost of a 3D print?
For FDM, material cost is filament weight in grams ÷ 1,000 × price per kilogram. For resin, use milliliters ÷ 1,000 × price per liter. Add print hours × average power in kilowatts × electricity rate, plus hourly machine cost. This calculator divides those print costs by (1 − failure rate), then adds labor and other costs for one successful part.
What should I enter for machine cost per hour?
One approach is (purchase price − expected resale value) ÷ expected lifetime printing hours, plus a maintenance allowance per hour. For example, a $600 machine with $100 resale value over 2,000 printing hours costs $0.25 per hour before maintenance. This is an example, not a universal machine rate. Electricity is entered separately to avoid double-counting.
Does this work for resin printers too?
Yes. Select Resin / SLA and enter resin volume in mL and bottle price per liter. If your resin is priced by kilogram, divide that price by volume in liters to get a per-liter price; check the manufacturer's density or stated bottle volume. Include supports in material use and account for washing, curing, gloves and cleaning supplies in labor and other costs.
How are failed prints accounted for?
A 10% failure rate implies 1 ÷ 0.9, or about 1.111 attempts per successful part. We apply that factor to material, electricity and machine time. This assumes failed attempts consume a full attempt's print resources, so it may be conservative for early failures. Labor and other costs are charged once per successful part; add any extra failure-handling work yourself.
What about multiple parts on the same build plate?
All inputs are per finished part. For a shared build plate, divide the total material and print time by the number of finished parts before entering them. Allocate setup labor the same way. The quantity control multiplies the resulting per-part estimate; it does not simulate build-plate efficiency or bulk discounts. Fixed selling fees are also charged per item.
Are my estimates private? What does sharing include?
Calculations run in your browser and your latest inputs are saved on this device when local storage is available. No print files are uploaded. Share estimate creates a link containing your calculator settings in its URL fragment, so anyone receiving it can read those settings. Quote names are excluded from shared links.
